MALAWI VACCINATES OVER 1.3 MILLION CHILDREN IN EMERGENCY POLIO DRIVE

In an urgent response to the detection of a new polio virus case in Blantyre, Malawi’s Ministry of Health, with support from the World Health Organization (WHO), has successfully vaccinated more than 1.3 million children in a four-day immunization campaign aimed at stopping the spread of the virus.

The vaccination drive one of the most rapid in recent memory was launched after environmental surveillance detected circulating poliovirus in Blantyre. This prompted immediate action to protect children across eight districts in the country’s Southern Region.

Dr Charles Chilambula, Deputy Minister of Health and Sanitation said that Polio is a debilitating disease that can leave one with lifetime disability and sometimes death. But the good news is that it’s preventable through vaccination, good personal hygiene and sanitation.

The emergency effort achieved impressive coverage, with Blantyre District reporting more than 100 % coverage due to return visits and outreach efforts to reach unvaccinated children. Social mobilization teams, religious leaders and community volunteers played a critical role in reassuring hesitant families and explaining the benefits of immunization.

WHO Representatives also praised the cooperative effort. Dr Charles Njuguna, WHO Representative in Malawi, described the campaign as an aggressive and well-coordinated effort to interrupt polio transmission.

Despite these gains, health officials emphasized that continued vigilance is necessary. Additional vaccination rounds are planned to reach the remaining children who missed the first phase of the campaign.

Health experts warn that poliovirus even in its vaccine-derived form can spread quietly in areas with low immunization coverage, potentially causing permanent paralysis in susceptible children. The Ministry of Health and partners are urging parents to bring children for follow-up vaccinations and continue routine immunizations at local clinics.
